About Highlands

Highland cattle are the oldest registered breed in the world, shaped over centuries in the Scottish hills. They came up hardy, patient, and good-natured — which turns out to be exactly what a family farm wants.

Built for weather, all of it

That famous double coat — a long, oiled outer layer over a soft down — was made for cold, wet Highland winters. What surprised us is how well it works in Mississippi. They shed it out for summer and it breathes in the heat, and come winter they barely notice the cold. They forage well on rough ground and turn modest pasture into good condition.

The temperament is the whole thing

Ask anyone who keeps Highlands and they'll tell you the same: raised with a little attention, they become genuinely gentle, curious, people-loving animals. Ours come to the fence to say hello. The cows are wonderful mothers. Even the bulls, ours included, are calm enough to scratch behind the horns.

They're the reason we stopped thinking of this as livestock and started thinking of it as a fold.

Small calves, easy calving

Highland calves come small and get up quick, which makes for low-stress calving and a very high success rate. They grow slowly and naturally, and that unhurried pace is a lot of why the breed is prized.

Why registered matters

Every animal in our fold is registered with the American Highland Cattle Association. Papers protect the breed's heritage, verify the bloodlines behind your animal, and mean their calves can be registered too. If you're building something to last, start with recorded stock.
