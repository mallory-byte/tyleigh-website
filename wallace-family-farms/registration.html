Registration & papers

Registration sounds like bureaucracy and mostly isn't. It's simply the breed keeping an honest family tree — and it's one of the real reasons to buy from a farm that does it properly.

What registration is

The American Highland Cattle Association keeps the herd book: the official record of Highland cattle in this country and the ancestry behind each one. A registered animal has a certificate with its registration number, date of birth, color, and the sire and dam it came from — a verifiable pedigree, not just our word.

Why it matters to you

  • Proof of what you bought. Papers confirm the bloodline and purity behind your animal.
  • Your calves can be registered too. Two registered parents mean registrable offspring — essential if you ever plan to breed or sell.
  • Resale value. Recorded stock holds its value in a way unregistered animals simply don't.
  • You're helping protect the breed. Every registration keeps the herd book — and centuries of careful breeding — intact.

How the transfer works

When you buy a registered animal from us, we sign the certificate over and file the transfer so the registration lands in your name. You'll receive the updated paperwork for your records. For a calf born after purchase, we'll walk you through registering it yourself — it's straightforward, and we're glad to help the first time.

Papers don't make an animal gentle or sound. But they make sure the good animal in front of you is exactly who we say she is.
