country life

The farm

The Highlands are the heart of the place, but they're not the whole of it. A working farm keeps a lot of company, and everyone here earns their keep one way or another — even the ones whose only job is to be charming.

The hens

A ragtag flock of laying hens has the run of the barnyard, trailing the cattle to pick through the pasture and turning up at the back door around suppertime. They give us more eggs than we can eat and more opinions than we asked for. In spring there are always a few broody ones tucked into corners, and by summer a scatter of chicks underfoot.

The guardians

Two Great Pyrenees, big as snowbanks and twice as calm, sleep in the pasture all day and work all night. They were raised with the calves and take the job personally. You'll rarely hear them — until you do, at two in the morning, letting a coyote know exactly where the property line is.

The goats

A handful of Nigerian Dwarf goats keep the fence lines and the brush honest, and keep us laughing the rest of the time. They are escape artists, comedians, and shameless beggars, and the children would trade none of them for anything.

The rhythm

We do this slow, on purpose. Mornings start with a walk of the fence and a head count. Evenings end the same way. In between there's grass to move them onto, water to check, and the ordinary small work that adds up to animals who are healthy, calm, and well known to us. It isn't an operation. It's a way of living, and we're grateful for it.
